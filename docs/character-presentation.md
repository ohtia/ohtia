# Character presentation API

## Ownership and inputs

The renderer exports `CharacterController`, `CharacterDecoration`, `CharacterPreview`, `CharacterDrawing`, `VoiceLabel` and `ContributorAttribution`. The [identity contract](agents/agent-identity.md) owns meaning and attention rules; the [design system](design-system.md#ohtias-symbol-and-character) owns geometry and poses. The components implement the presentation boundary of [STORY-002-008](epics/EPIC-002-basic-interface/STORY-002-008-character-presentation.md), preserving researcher control and attribution under PRIN-002, PRIN-004, PRIN-006 and PRIN-007.

Supply the latest confirmed `character` record from the window-owned `SettingsController`, using `static` before the preference store is available. Draft Settings choices are preview inputs only. `CharacterDecoration` observes system reduced motion throughout its mounted lifetime and synchronously cancels registered motion when the OS override changes. The [named preference operations](application-port.md) retain ownership of persistence, receipts, revisions and cross-window publication; the decoration controller has no application port or storage interface.

Create one `CharacterController` for a workspace session and dispose it when that session closes. Its surface starts unavailable; `CharacterDecoration` attaches and detaches it, and arrivals before attachment remain static without a presentation timer. Pass its controller, confirmed mode and current manuscript visibility to `CharacterDecoration`. In React composition, that prop is the single writer of committed manuscript visibility; request `openNavigator` from an effect after the actual Navigator opening and visibility change commit. Do not preemptively publish a different visibility through the controller while the prop still describes a visible manuscript. Non-React surface adapters publish their actual visibility directly. Keep notices, actions and retained contributions in consumer-owned read models outside the decorative component. The controller's `notice` is the selected presentation notice, not a research-history repository or a queue of all received notices. During a gesture its selected notice remains in place; the consumer keeps subsequent notices available statically. A manuscript-visible or sub-96-pixel surface uses compact static glasses. Static events received beside a visible manuscript remain compact after it closes, with no delayed full illustration. Hidden mode also keeps the glasses while omitting the full drawing. These drawings are decorative and excluded from the accessibility tree.

## Controlled events and cancellation

`receive` accepts an immutable event identity, project, originating action/result/batch, actual producer or unknown, relevance, text with its actual language, delivered pose and explicit deferred-Navigator eligibility. This is a controlled consumer contract, not a decoder of provider prose. The first consuming screen must establish committed relevance and select its permitted binding before calling it. Passive reads in an existing workspace use `receive(event, true)` and remain static while retaining a coincident unused deferred allowance. History restored after restart or a resumed session uses `restore(event)`, which cancels presentation and consumes that history batch regardless of manuscript visibility or attachment. A resumed live receipt cannot obtain a new allowance from the restored batch; only an explicit new action or newly opened project session can supply a new batch. The application composition supplies no research-event bindings in this increment.

The delivered drawing repertoire is Thinking, Disagreement and Milestone, plus neutral branding. Their durations are 1600, 1800 and 520 milliseconds. Event decoration shows the full illustration only during its bounded `presenting` interval and returns to compact glasses after expiration or dismissal. A switch to Static cancels motion immediately and starts a bounded main-pose interval no longer than the pose's normal duration; returning to Animated cancels that still interval. Main static poses retain the specified head, eyebrow and mouth treatment; Milestone's static fallback stays at rest. Pupil offsets stay inside the 4.5-unit limit. The full illustration preserves the canonical frame, glasses, coat, curls, bun, pencil and piece order. The event stage reserves a spacing-token guard around the drawing so the Milestone jump does not clip the bun or pencil or overlap neighboring controls. `CharacterPreview` retains the density-aware static branding asset at the Settings sizes; it never starts an event animation.

Event identities are consumed once per workspace lifetime, including delivery under another batch. One batch can supply one new full presentation, animated or still; a mode override may continue the active event as its bounded static fallback. Later receipts from a consumed batch remain compact in the consumer's notice collection. If a still presentation is already running, those receipts retain its selected notice and original deadline rather than removing or extending the still interval; a genuinely new batch can select a new presentation. Events arriving during motion stay static in the consumer's notice collection and do not form an animation backlog. A valid later batch arriving while the workspace is free remains eligible; an arrival during motion does not reserve a future gesture. A manuscript-visible eligible event can retain one deferred identity and its original batch. A coincident live receipt in the pending batch cannot bypass that explicit opening after passive manuscript hiding. Explicit consumption selects the original deferred notice; it does not replace the consumer-owned collection of later static notices. `openNavigator(true, stillRelevant)` consumes it only after the consumer has explicitly opened the Navigator and removed manuscript visibility. Passive Navigator restoration does not consume the deferred animation allowance; a passive receipt still consumes its event identity. Manuscript-visible passive receipts in the current session preserve an unused batch allowance so eligible coincident live receipts work in either order. Already consumed batches stay consumed. History and resumed records use `restore`, which always consumes their original batch. This is a recovery boundary that cancels any prior pending presentation, including one from another batch; ordinary current-session history reads use passive `receive` instead. A passive receipt without a visible manuscript or matching pending deferral consumes its batch as static presentation. Dismissal, resolution, renderer replacement, project closure, history recovery, nonanimated modes and a superseding new-action gesture invalidate the pending presentation and consume its original batch; returning to Animated cannot revive it.

`invalidate(id)` dismisses or resolves one event; `invalidate()` clears the current presentation. `cancelPresentation()` cancels decorative work while retaining its notice text. Detaching a renderer marks its surface unavailable and cancels presentation. A replacement waits for its own attachment and rechecks the current event before creating motion, so it cannot replay the previous renderer's event from a stale render snapshot. `setSurfaceAvailable(false)` also consumes events suppressed while a surface is compact or absent; restoring space cannot queue them for later. `dispose()` cancels timers and animation handles and removes subscriptions. Callbacks retain their original event, batch and lifetime epoch; canceled or disposed callbacks cannot animate a replacement event. An injected `CharacterClock` supports deterministic tests without granting consumer timers storage or research authority.

## Static attribution

`VoiceLabel` accepts an artifact-owned identity and interface locale. Ohtia uses glasses; Virgilio, Josefa, Jorge Luis, Erasmus, Boris and María use distinct static role motifs and localized role labels with explicit AI origin. Boris and María also identify Writing review and their distinct modes. Symbols preserve the normative per-piece geometry and stroke treatment in the [Josefa symbol](assets/agents/josefa/symbol.svg) and the other role symbol files. No specialist full portraits or contributor-selection operation is introduced. The original human-supervisor variant carries the declared author and submitted manuscript version, uses `user-round` and the human voice, and must never label an AI extraction or interpretation. Unknown identities remain unknown.

`ContributorAttribution` separates the identity and role-definition version from the actual artifact producer, observed provider, observed model and translated execution text. Missing observations display Unknown. Producer/provider/model and version values are names or identifiers, not translated research prose; their unknown language remains separate from localized labels and execution text. These text fields do not establish readiness, independent examination or human confirmation. Store historical identity with the contribution rather than deriving it from a selected contributor. Render artifact text separately with its recorded language and `dir="auto"`; changing interface language localizes role labels without relabeling or translating historical research content.

Labels wrap at every width; compact symbols remain still in grayscale, Hidden mode and narrow layouts. Product/AI labels use `voice.ohtia.content`; original supervisor labels use `voice.tutor.content`; unknown labels use `content.primary`. The checked color inventory covers their supported surfaces. No icon, color or portrait supplies indispensable information without text. These components add no focusable controls, live announcements, dialogs or keyboard shortcuts; their consumer owns notice/action semantics and any accepted-outcome announcement.
